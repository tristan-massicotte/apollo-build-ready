1. Accuracy compounds by kit

A kit is build-ready only if every one of its records is right.
37%of 100-part kits fully right at 99% per part
99.9%per part to get 9 kits in 10 right
~1 in 1,000wrong records allowed across part numbers at that level

Kit right = (per-part accuracy) ^ (parts in the kit). Real errors cluster (same location, same receiver), which can move the number either way. The kit size is my assumption.

2. Where records go wrong

Receiving, two inventories after the CM transfer, and engineering changes.

The material flow from prototype to production, as Apptronik's postings describe it. Highlighted steps are my hypotheses.

  1. Watch
    Receiving and stock
    Every miss has one of three causes: never received, wrong location, or used without a transaction. Counting the parts that stop builds most often, most often (ABC), finds them before a kit is released.
  2. Watch
    Contract manufacturer transfer
    Your postings mention "Contract Manufacturer technical transfer". After it, there are two inventories (yours and the CM's), consigned parts, and two systems to keep in sync.
  3. Kitting by subassembly ("Build Ready")
  4. Watch
    Engineering changes (ECO / MCO)
    From prototype builds through production ramp, each change makes some records wrong until it is processed: old revisions on the shelf, new ones not yet received.
  5. Assembly and test

My 16 weeks

  1. Wk 1–2Count before I change anything. Shifts in receiving, the stockroom and the line; count every part of one subassembly and measure its real record accuracy as a baseline.
  2. Wk 3–6Own Build Ready for that subassembly. A check before every kit release; each miss gets a root cause (never received, wrong location, unrecorded use) and a fix.
  3. Wk 7–11Set up cycle counting. ABC classes, count frequencies and location control with the inventory and procurement team, and map what moves to the contract manufacturer.
  4. Wk 12–16Run it and prove it. Measure accuracy and build stops against the week-2 baseline, fix what breaks, then take on a second subassembly.
